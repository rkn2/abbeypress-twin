Saint Vincent College · Latrobe, Pennsylvania · about 1900 to 2026

The Abbey Press building, Saint Vincent

The Abbey Press building stood beside the main entrance to Saint Vincent College in Latrobe for more than a century. It was built around 1900 as the Archabbey's printing office, bindery and carpenter shop, and it came down in fall 2026, before our team could document it in person. This page tells its story from the maps, aerial photos and reports that survive, and lets you explore a 3D model rebuilt from them.

About 1900

Built for the press

Saint Vincent's monks bought their first printing presses in 1864, decades before this building existed. The 1887 fire insurance map shows the site empty. By 1903 the same map shows a two-storey brick building shaped like a T, with printing on the ground floor, a book bindery above it, an engine room in the taller centre and a carpenter shop in the north wing.

It was probably built between 1897 and 1903, not in the 1840s as was sometimes said. The monastery ran its own brickyard nearby at the time, although no record ties these bricks to it.

1903 to 2020

A century by the entrance

The building shows up in every aerial photo of the campus from 1939 on. It stood apart from the central buildings that burned in 1963 and went on working as a shop. In recent years it held carpentry machinery, an old printing press in one corner and Br. Mark Floreanini's stained-glass studio.

The building is at the centre of each frame. PennPilot and NHAP aerial photos, via PASDA.

March 2021

Two assessments that disagree

In March 2021 two reports looked at the building. MCF Architects found the outer brick walls sound apart from repointing and loose parapets, but the wood floors inside were failing and the building was unsafe to occupy. A second list, probably from college facilities, described crumbling brick and leaning parapets.

MCF put a full renovation at $9.9 to 11 million. The two reports disagree about the walls, and this record keeps both readings on the model.

Fall 2026

Demolition

In fall 2026 donors to Penn State's College of Arts and Architecture asked the university to help save the building, and a group of faculty began planning a visit to scan and photograph it. By the time one of them reached the site, it was already coming down.

The last aerial photo that shows it standing was taken on September 2, 2026. Everything below is built from what was recorded before then.

Model

Explore the model

We rebuilt the building from public lidar, the fire insurance maps and the aerial photos. The west front is placed from photos, and where no photo shows a wall its windows are our estimate. You can see which is which under Layers, then Colour, then geometry source. Click any wall, roof or window to see the condition records and sources behind it, or switch to the aerial photos under Layers. Saint Vincent College never confirmed for us which building this was, so the identification rests on the maps and photos.
